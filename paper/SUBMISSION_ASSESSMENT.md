# Submission assessment

The first manuscript is complete and reviewable. Its strongest contribution is a reproducible **evaluation methodology and empirical audit**, rather than a new network or a claim that no useful router can exist. Journal acceptance and quartile fit cannot be established by writing quality alone.

## What is now supported

The paper provides an exact oracle implementation, linear-program validation, 90 seed-transfer comparisons across 15 architectures, cost-matched contrasts, policy-selection sensitivity bounds, a disjoint learned-gate analysis, seven raw-verified joint controls, and immutable provenance. The main results correct and extend the earlier project narrative. Authors are empty as requested.

## What would materially strengthen a selective journal submission

1. Evaluate a stronger representation- or trajectory-based sequential router with a genuinely independent calibration and evaluation dataset. The simple logistic gate cannot support a broad conclusion about learnability.
2. Measure cumulative exit-head, gate and device execution costs. The current prefix-cost proxy cannot establish practical speedups.
3. Extend the seed-transfer/envelope analysis to an external dataset and an official multi-exit architecture recipe, with more than one joint seed per architecture.
4. Obtain a domain expert's novelty and mathematical review, especially for related oracle-allocation and routing-evaluation work. The current manuscript deliberately makes no priority claim.

These are strengthening experiments, not silently claimed completed work. The manuscript explains its present scope so it can be reviewed honestly now.
